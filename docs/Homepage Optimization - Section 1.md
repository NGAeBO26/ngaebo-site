# North Georgia E-Bikes Homepage Section 1 Handoff — v2

## Status
Consolidated after the latest direction from Jeff and the combined recommendations from Launch Marketing Planner + Copy Partner.

## Section name
Homepage Section 1: Hero / Top Fold

## Section job
Move first-time visitors into **RideBuilder** by making it feel like the fastest, smartest way to choose a better North Georgia ride for today.

## Primary user takeaway
“This helps me choose a ride that fits today’s conditions before I waste time on the wrong route.”

## Conversion priorities
1. Primary CTA: drive visitors into RideBuilder
2. Secondary CTA: offer 3 free sample RideGuides as the backup path
3. Reinforce why unlocking a RideGuide is worth it without turning the hero into a price pitch

## Product framing to preserve
- **RideAtlas** = the map application
- **RideBuilder** = the route-matching experience inside RideAtlas
- **RideGuide** = the route-specific digital guide unlocked after using RideBuilder

---

## Layout

### Desktop
Use a 2-column hero.

**Left column**
1. Eyebrow / brand tagline
2. Headline
3. Supporting copy
4. CTA row
5. Trust / proof line
6. Rider-value bridge line
7. Optional short value reassurance line

**Right column**
1. Hero product visual
2. 3 proof labels tied to rider outcomes

### Mobile
Stack in this order:
1. Eyebrow / tagline
2. Headline
3. Supporting copy
4. Primary CTA
5. Secondary CTA
6. Hero visual
7. Trust / proof line
8. Rider-value bridge line
9. Optional short value reassurance line
10. Proof labels

### Hierarchy notes
- Keep the headline and primary CTA visible quickly.
- Make the primary CTA clearly dominant.
- Keep the secondary CTA visibly available but subordinate.
- Do not surface bikes, gear, or shopping CTAs in this section.
- The visual should feel like proof, not decoration.

## Final recommended copy

### Eyebrow / tagline
**Plan Faster. Ride Smarter.**

### Headline
**Find the Right North Georgia Ride for Today’s Conditions — Before You Roll Out**

### Supporting copy
Use **RideBuilder** inside **RideAtlas** to match your bike, your time, and the kind of ride you want to a route that fits today. Then unlock a **RideGuide** with current-weather-powered ride intelligence so you can ride safer, avoid surprises, and know what to expect before you go.

### Primary CTA
**Find My Ride with RideBuilder**

### Secondary CTA
**See 3 Free Sample RideGuides**

### Trust / proof line
**Built for North Georgia riders • Current-weather-powered ride intelligence • No subscription required**

### Rider-value bridge line
**Know Before You Go.** See when to ride, how hard it’ll feel, and what to watch before you commit.

### Optional value reassurance line
**For $6.99, you get route-specific ride intelligence that can help you avoid the wrong ride, bad timing, and unnecessary guesswork.**

---

## Why this copy works
- **Safer:** it promises better visibility into route-specific risk before the rider commits
- **More enjoyable:** it helps riders choose a route that fits their bike, time, and energy
- **More predictable:** it highlights today’s conditions, expected effort, and what to watch for
- **Worth $6.99:** it frames the purchase as a small cost that can prevent a wasted trip, wrong-route choice, or underprepared ride

---

## Visual label guidance
Use rider-language labels in or around the proof panel:

- **Prime Ride Time** — Best window to ride this route
- **Effort Gauge** — How hard it will really feel today
- **Risk Radar** — What to watch before you go

If space is limited, keep the bold labels in the visual and move the explanations below the image.

---

## CTA behavior

### Primary CTA behavior
Send the visitor directly into **RideBuilder** inside **RideAtlas**.

### Secondary CTA behavior
Open the free-sample lead capture flow. Keep it visually subordinate so it feels like a fallback path, not the main action.

---

## Messaging rules for implementation
- Lead with **ride intelligence and rider outcomes**, not GIS or backend language
- Keep the copy centered on **why to start RideBuilder now**
- Make the hero answer these practical questions quickly:
  - Will this help me choose a safer ride?
  - Will this help me have a better ride today?
  - Will this help me avoid surprises?
  - Is this worth paying for?
- Do not make price the headline story
- Use price only as a short reassurance point
- Keep the copy tight enough that the section still feels fast and high-converting

---

## Developer notes
- Primary CTA should be a filled, highest-contrast button
- Secondary CTA should be ghost, outline, or text-link style
- Keep supporting copy to roughly 2 short lines on desktop if possible
- Keep the bridge line visually distinct from the body copy so it reads like proof, not a second paragraph
- If the optional value reassurance line makes the hero too dense, move it to the top of Section 2 as a short objection-handling block
- Avoid technical phrases like **GIS pipeline**, **spatial modeling**, or **physics engine** in the hero itself
